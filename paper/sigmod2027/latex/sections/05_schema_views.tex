\section{Dataset Schema and Views}
\label{sec:schema-views}

The canonical candidate schema currently consists of decision metadata, candidate identity, split information, labels, and feature columns. Each row corresponds to exactly one candidate victim at exactly one full-cache miss decision. Trace identity and split metadata are recorded through \texttt{trace\_name}, \texttt{trace\_family}, \texttt{dataset\_source}, and \texttt{split}. Cache configuration is recorded through \texttt{capacity} and \texttt{horizon}. Decision identifiers include \texttt{decision\_id} and \texttt{decision\_t}. The schema also records a per-decision chunk ID. That field is named \texttt{decision\_chunk\_id}. Candidate identity is represented through the field \texttt{candidate\_page\_id}. The labels are \texttt{y\_loss}, the finite-horizon counterfactual miss cost after forcing that candidate eviction under the fixed continuation policy, and \texttt{y\_value} $= -\texttt{y\_loss}$. Feature columns cover request context, candidate context, predictor/LRU signals, cache summary statistics, and recent request or hit rates.

Beyond candidate rows, the release defines two derived views. The \emph{decision view} aggregates all rows that share a decision identifier and summarizes each decision with candidate count, minimum loss, maximum value, the set of optimal candidate IDs, tie count, and regret summary statistics. The \emph{pairwise view} compares candidate pairs from the same decision and derives pairwise preference labels from the ordering of their \texttt{y\_loss} values, with ties when the losses are equal. The evaluated open release does not ship the full pairwise view; it ships a capped pairwise sample of 1,000,000 rows instead.

Operationally, the schema can be read as a layered projection over the same decision events: candidate rows are the atomic supervised objects, the decision view groups those rows by \texttt{decision\_id}, and the shipped pairwise sample keeps only selected within-decision comparisons.
