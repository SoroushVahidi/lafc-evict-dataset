\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Reuse}
\label{sec:limitations}

This benchmark has several important constraints. First, the main label is a finite-horizon continuation-policy-specific counterfactual target. It is decision-aligned for the benchmarked eviction choice, but it is not an offline-optimal label for the full future control problem. Good performance on this label therefore does not automatically imply online deployment gains.

Second, the labels depend on the continuation policy used after the forced eviction. A different continuation policy or a different horizon can change the numerical target, the optimal set within a decision, and the induced pairwise preferences. Comparisons are therefore meaningful only across artifacts that keep these label-generation choices fixed; the shipped pairwise sample is likewise capped and tie-heavy (Section~\ref{sec:tasks-baselines}).

Third, the open subset is intentionally narrower than the larger generated dataset mix. Two trace families remain excluded from the open subset for redistribution reasons, and not every selected family appears in every split in the evaluated release package. This release-governance boundary is part of the benchmark story and is made explicit rather than hidden in artifact packaging details.

Fourth, benchmark reuse must preserve provenance distinctions. The release exposes generated supervision and derived tasks, but it does not erase the attribution, citation, or licensing requirements of the upstream raw traces from which candidate rows were derived. Those raw traces remain external artifacts with their own governance constraints.

Fifth, the evaluated open-release snapshot is distinct from any later public distribution channel; the manuscript centers that validated release package and its reproducible validation evidence rather than final hosting details.

Finally, the generated labels are intended for offline benchmarking claims. They can support comparative modeling studies, but they are not automatic evidence that a learned policy is ready for operational deployment without separate online evaluation, systems integration, and safety review. The paper also does not report full-policy evaluations against external eviction algorithms such as SIEVE, S3-FIFO, MAT, or Parrot; those methods are relevant comparison points and future benchmark consumers, but they are not claimed as evaluated baselines here.
