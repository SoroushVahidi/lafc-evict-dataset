\begin{abstract}
\begin{sloppypar}
Evaluating cache-eviction decisions requires knowing what would have
happened under each candidate choice -- information not observable from a
single trace replay. This paper introduces \lafc, a reusable way to
construct that counterfactual evidence at scale: a per-decision,
per-candidate finite-horizon counterfactual miss-cost label, computed for
real cache traces, evaluated against five questions. \textbf{(RQ1)} Is the
target discriminative between candidates? Across five trace families,
four capacities, and three horizons, two-thirds of decisions are fully
tied in aggregate and no audited decision has a strict unique winner, but
discriminativeness varies strongly and predictably by workload, capacity,
and horizon, and one workload is a fully degenerate, mechanistically
explained negative control. \textbf{(RQ2)} Does the offline ranking
correspond to closed-loop cache performance? A closed-loop evaluation over
production-derived traces (LRU, MRU, random, SIEVE; five families, two
capacities, 20 seeds) shows offline and closed-loop policy orderings agree
in most regimes where the offline signal is not near zero, with two
characterized exceptions. \textbf{(RQ3)} Capacity-scale reuse predicts
both offline discriminativeness and closed-loop separation
($r=0.83$--$0.97$, $n=10$, exploratory), strengthening at longer horizons.
\textbf{(RQ4)} A pre-registered sampled study (5,500 decisions, capacities
32 and 128) and a separate full-population MRU census (capacities 32, 64,
128, and 256) both find the canonical LRU-continuation labels robust;
random continuation is measurably more perturbing than MRU, and no
population-scale random result is claimed. \textbf{(RQ5)} This offline
supervision complements rather than replaces closed-loop evaluation, and
we give concrete guidance for when it can be trusted. \lafc\ also releases
the underlying candidate-, decision-, and pairwise-level supervision for
reuse, with every quantitative claim traced to an independently
validated, gate-checked artifact.
\end{sloppypar}
\end{abstract}
