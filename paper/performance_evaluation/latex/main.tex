\begin{abstract}
\begin{sloppypar}
A single trace replay evicts one candidate per decision and so never
reveals what would have happened under the alternatives -- the
counterfactual information needed to actually evaluate an eviction choice.
This paper introduces \lafc, a reusable way to construct that
counterfactual evidence at scale: for every resident candidate at every
full-cache miss, a finite-horizon counterfactual miss-cost label, computed
for real cache traces across five workload families, four cache
capacities, and three horizons. We evaluate this supervision surface
against five questions. \textbf{(RQ1: is the target discriminative?)}
Not uniformly: two-thirds of decisions are fully tied in aggregate and no
audited decision has a strict unique winner, but discriminativeness varies
strongly and predictably by workload, capacity, and horizon, and one
workload is a fully degenerate, mechanistically explained negative
control. \textbf{(RQ2: does the offline ranking correspond to closed-loop
performance?)} We ran a closed-loop evaluation of LRU, MRU, random, and
SIEVE across the five families at two capacities (20 random seeds).
Offline and closed-loop policy orderings agree in most regimes where the
offline signal is not near zero, with two characterized exceptions.
\textbf{(RQ3: what drives this correspondence?)} Capacity-scale reuse
(trace-derived LRU hit rate) correlates with both offline
discriminativeness ($r=0.83$) and closed-loop separation ($r=0.90$), and
discriminativeness and closed-loop separation correlate with each other
($r=0.97$) (all $n=10$, exploratory). The correspondence is strongest at
$H=16$, the longest of the three evaluated horizons ($H\in\{4,8,16\}$);
this is not evidence about horizons beyond 16. \textbf{(RQ4: how sensitive
are the labels to the continuation policy assumed after a forced
eviction?)} A pre-registered sampled study (5,500 decisions, capacities 32
and 128) and a full-population MRU census (capacities 32, 64, 128, and
256) both find the canonical LRU-continuation labels robust to the tested
alternatives. Random continuation is measurably more perturbing than MRU;
we make no population-scale claim for random continuation. \textbf{(RQ5:
what should this mean in practice?)} This offline supervision complements
rather than replaces closed-loop evaluation, and we give concrete guidance
for when it can be trusted. \lafc\ also releases the underlying candidate-,
decision-, and pairwise-level supervision for reuse, with every
quantitative claim traced to an independently validated, gate-checked
artifact.
\end{sloppypar}
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
\begin{sloppypar}
The \lafc\ repository is public at
\url{https://github.com/SoroushVahidi/lafc-evict-dataset}. Code in the
repository is released under the MIT License; the dataset itself is
released under CC0-1.0 per its \texttt{CITATION.cff} dataset-metadata
record. Upstream trace governance is separate from both: alibaba-block,
metacdn, metakv, and twemcache remain uncleared for public derived-data
redistribution under their own source licensing/usage terms, independent
of this repository's own code and dataset licenses, so they are
represented here through validated summary/provenance artifacts rather
than a public download of their generated rows. The current public
derived-data snapshot (v0.3) is wiki2018-only and is distributed through
Hugging Face
(\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/SoroushVahidi/lafc-evict}) and AWS
Open Data. Zenodo currently archives v0.2 only (version DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21895844}{10.5281/zenodo.21895844};
