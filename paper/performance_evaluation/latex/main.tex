\begin{abstract}
\begin{sloppypar}
Evaluating cache-eviction decisions requires knowing what would have
happened under each candidate choice -- information not observable from a
single trace replay. This paper introduces \lafc, a reusable way to
construct that counterfactual evidence at scale: a per-decision,
per-candidate finite-horizon counterfactual miss-cost label, computed for
real cache traces, evaluated against five questions. \textbf{(RQ1)} Is the
target discriminative between candidates? Across five trace families,
four capacities, and three horizons, two-thirds of decisions are fully
tied in aggregate and no audited decision has a strict unique winner, but
discriminativeness varies strongly and predictably by workload, capacity,
and horizon, and one workload is a fully degenerate, mechanistically
explained negative control. \textbf{(RQ2)} Does the offline ranking
correspond to closed-loop cache performance? A closed-loop evaluation over
production-derived traces (LRU, MRU, random, SIEVE; five families, two
capacities, 20 seeds) shows offline and closed-loop policy orderings agree
in most regimes where the offline signal is not near zero, with two
characterized exceptions. \textbf{(RQ3)} Capacity-scale reuse correlates with
both offline discriminativeness and closed-loop separation
($r=0.83$--$0.97$, $n=10$, exploratory); this correlation is strongest at
the longest of the three main-release horizons ($H=16$ of
$H\in\{4,8,16\}$), while a separate H32--H128 characterization audit shows
that longer horizons increase informativeness in reuse-heavy workloads
without eliminating low-information regimes.
\textbf{(RQ4)} A pre-registered sampled study (5,500 decisions, capacities
32 and 128) and a separate full-population MRU census (capacities 32, 64,
128, and 256) both find the canonical LRU-continuation labels robust;
random continuation is measurably more perturbing than MRU, and no
population-scale random result is claimed. \textbf{(RQ5)} This offline
supervision complements rather than replaces closed-loop evaluation, and
we give concrete guidance for when it can be trusted. \lafc\ also releases
the underlying candidate-, decision-, and pairwise-level supervision for
reuse, with every quantitative claim traced to an independently
validated, gate-checked artifact.
\end{sloppypar}
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
\begin{sloppypar}
The \lafc\ repository is public at
\url{https://github.com/SoroushVahidi/lafc-evict-dataset}; repository code
is released under MIT, and the derived dataset is separately multi-licensed
by trace family (see below and \texttt{THIRD\_PARTY\_DATA.md}). The
scientific evaluation in this paper uses a five-family corpus
(alibaba-block, metacdn, metakv, twemcache, wiki2018); all five families
are cleared for public derived-data redistribution under their respective
upstream licenses (CC0-1.0 for wiki2018; CC~BY~4.0 for alibaba-block and
twemcache; Apache License 2.0 for metacdn and metakv). The exact evaluated
corpus (277{,}995{,}072 candidate rows; 2{,}363{,}286 decisions; plus a
1{,}000{,}000-row canonical pairwise sample) is publicly downloadable on
Hugging Face as release \texttt{v1.0}
(\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/SoroushVahidi/lafc-evict/tree/v1.0},
revision \texttt{37173bc96de2a615455bf9713bb90d846156af29}), independently
verified after publication by anonymous, unauthenticated download and
checksum comparison against the locally recorded manifest. A wiki2018-only
v0.3 snapshot (22{,}356{,}992 rows) remains separately published, unchanged,
on the same repository's \texttt{main} branch/revision and on AWS Open
Data. Neither \texttt{v0.3} nor \texttt{v1.0} has yet been archived on
Zenodo, which currently serves only the earlier \texttt{v0.2} release
(version DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21895844}{10.5281/zenodo.21895844};
concept DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21895843}{10.5281/zenodo.21895843});
do not cite this concept DOI as identifying \texttt{v1.0}. Brightkite and
citibike were excluded from data collection, remain blocked pending
license/privacy review, and are not part of the evaluated benchmark or any
release.
\end{sloppypar}
