\begin{abstract}
\begin{sloppypar}
Learned cache-eviction research has outgrown small ad hoc evaluations, yet empirical comparison remains difficult because prior studies often rely on private traces, release-incomplete preprocessing pipelines, or supervision targets that are not comparable across papers. This paper presents \lafc, a benchmark package for learned cache eviction in caching systems built around decision-aligned counterfactual supervision. Each canonical candidate row represents one candidate victim considered at one full-cache miss decision and includes release-facing provenance fields, cache-state features, and a finite-horizon counterfactual label. The canonical loss label, \texttt{y\_loss}, is the continuation-policy miss cost over a finite horizon after forcing that candidate eviction. The corresponding value label, \texttt{y\_value}, is defined as $-\texttt{y\_loss}$. The benchmark also exposes decision-level and pairwise benchmark views to support value prediction, best-candidate selection, pairwise preference learning, and label-surface characterization.

The evaluated open-release snapshot covers five selected trace families---cloudphysics, metacdn, metakv, twemcache, and wiki2018---and its release metadata record 277,995,072 candidate rows, 2,363,286 decision-view rows, and a capped pairwise sample of 1,000,000 rows. Two additional families, brightkite and citibike, remain excluded from the open subset. Beyond benchmark definition, release schema, validation, and reproducibility, the paper reports full candidate-label summaries, a linear \texttt{y\_loss} regression baseline, a best-candidate selector induced by that linear score, and pairwise-sample-backed feature baselines. Across all candidate rows, \texttt{y\_loss} has mean 7.7561, standard deviation 4.6542, and 5/25/50/75/95th percentiles of 2/4/7/11/16; the mirrored \texttt{y\_value} summary confirms the sign-flipped construction. The linear regression baseline is intentionally weak (test MAE 3.8739, RMSE 4.7152, $R^2=0.0026$), while the derived best-candidate selector reaches 0.8753 optimal-selection rate with 0.1251 mean regret. The shipped pairwise sample's A/B orientation is assigned by a deterministic hash over stable decision and pair-identity fields rather than by candidate-identifier ordering, giving a balanced non-tie split (about 49.8\%/50.2\%); on the resulting non-tie subset (121,738 of 1,000,000 rows), a logistic feature-difference baseline reaches 0.9660 accuracy with 0.0824 log loss after joining released candidate features back to the capped sample for evaluation only. These results demonstrate benchmark executability rather than online policy quality, and they are not evidence of online-policy superiority. The labels are therefore interpreted as decision-aligned finite-horizon counterfactual targets rather than offline-optimal supervision.
\end{sloppypar}
\end{abstract}
