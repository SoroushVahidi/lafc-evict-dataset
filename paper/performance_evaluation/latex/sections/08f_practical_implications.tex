\section{Practical Implications (RQ5)}
\label{sec:practical-implications}

This section synthesizes Sections~\ref{sec:characterization}--\ref{sec:continuation}
into guidance, without turning exploratory correlations into universal
rules.

\textbf{When should the offline counterfactual signal be trusted?} Most
directly in workload/capacity regimes with high capacity-scale reuse --
identifiable, before running any closed-loop replay, from a low trace-derived
all-tied fraction or a high trace-derived LRU hit rate
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). In this dataset that means metacdn- and
twemcache-like regimes, where offline discriminativeness is high and
offline$\leftrightarrow$closed-loop agreement is strongest.

\textbf{When should a researcher be cautious?} In near-zero-offline-signal
regimes (cloudphysics/cap32 is the concrete example here): a small offline
regret gap can flip sign without indicating a real disagreement -- it is
consistent with, not evidence against, offline informativeness elsewhere.
Caution is also warranted around cold-start-scored windows (MetaKV) and
validation-window-only evidence (MetaCDN), both stated explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:experimental-methodology} rather than left implicit.

\textbf{What does a high tie rate mean?} Not that the target is useless --
Section~\ref{sec:characterization}'s aggregate all-tied fraction of 0.68
coexists with genuinely discriminative sub-regimes. A high tie rate is
itself informative: it identifies where a policy comparison is unlikely to
be resolvable by this label family at all (wiki2018 is the limiting case),
which is useful information for deciding where \emph{not} to expect a
learned method to outperform a heuristic on this evidence.

\textbf{Why is closed-loop evaluation still necessary?} Because the
offline$\leftrightarrow$closed-loop correspondence in
Section~\ref{sec:linkage} is real but bounded: it holds in most, not all,
regimes, rests on $n=8$--10 independent cells, and has at least one
exception (metakv/cap128) that is not explained by existing evidence. A
result derived only from offline labels cannot be presented as a
closed-loop performance claim.

\textbf{How should continuation-policy assumptions be interpreted?} As a
tested, not assumed, robustness property, and one with real limits: the
labels are close to unaffected by switching to MRU continuation at
population scale across capacities 32/64/128/256, more (though still
boundedly) affected by random continuation in the validated sample at
capacities 32/128, and the sampled random effect grows somewhat with
horizon (Section~\ref{sec:continuation}). This supports using the
canonical LRU-continuation labels with reasonable confidence in the
evaluated regimes, not a claim that continuation choice is irrelevant.

\textbf{What capacity-scale reuse tells you before running anything.} The
single trace statistic most predictive of both offline informativeness and
closed-loop policy separation in this evaluation is a simple, cheaply
computed one -- trace-derived LRU hit rate at the target capacity
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}) -- which a practitioner can compute on a new
trace before committing to either an offline-label study or a full
closed-loop evaluation of it.

None of the correlations underlying this guidance ($r=0.6$--$0.97$
depending on which one) are treated here as anything stronger than the
$n=8$--10 exploratory findings they are; they inform where to look next,
not a guarantee of what will be found there.
