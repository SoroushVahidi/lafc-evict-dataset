\section{Practical Implications (RQ5)}
\label{sec:practical-implications}

This section synthesizes Sections~\ref{sec:characterization}--\ref{sec:continuation}
into guidance, without turning exploratory correlations into universal
rules.

\textbf{When should the offline counterfactual signal be trusted?} Most
directly in workload/capacity regimes with positive decision
informativeness and high capacity-scale reuse -- identifiable, before
running any closed-loop replay, from low all-tied mass, larger expected
random regret, or a high trace-derived LRU hit rate
(Sections~\ref{sec:characterization} and~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). In this
dataset that means metacdn- and twemcache-like regimes for label
informativeness, although the highest-information twemcache slices also
show that LRU-like features are not always enough. The benchmark is
therefore most useful for regime-conditioned comparison, not as a single
pooled leaderboard.

\textbf{When should a researcher be cautious?} In near-zero-offline-signal
regimes (alibaba-block/cap32 is the concrete example here): a small offline
regret gap can flip sign without indicating a real disagreement -- it is
consistent with, not evidence against, offline informativeness elsewhere.
Caution is also warranted around cold-start-scored windows (MetaKV) and
validation-window-only evidence (MetaCDN), both stated explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:experimental-methodology} rather than left implicit.

\textbf{What does a high tie rate mean?} Not that the target is useless --
Section~\ref{sec:characterization}'s aggregate all-tied fraction of 0.68
coexists with genuinely discriminative sub-regimes. A high tie rate is
itself informative: it identifies where a policy comparison is unlikely to
be resolvable by this label family at all (wiki2018 is the limiting case),
which is useful information for deciding where \emph{not} to expect a
learned method to outperform a heuristic on this evidence. Conversely, when
expected random regret is positive, regret rather than raw optimal-set rate
is the safer primary comparison: LRU and the clean pairwise selector
separate from random in all positive-information strata, while weak
pointwise learned selectors are exposed as worse than random.

\textbf{Why still run closed-loop evaluation?} The offline/closed-loop
association in Section~\ref{sec:linkage} is real but bounded: it holds in
most, not all, regimes, rests on $n=8$--10 cells, and has at least one
unexplained exception (metakv/cap128). Offline-label results therefore
cannot be presented as closed-loop performance claims.

\textbf{How should continuation-policy assumptions be interpreted?} As a
tested, not assumed, robustness property, and one with real limits: the
labels are close to unaffected by switching to MRU continuation at
population scale across capacities 32/64/128/256, more (though still
boundedly) affected by random continuation in the validated sample at
capacities 32/128, and the sampled random effect grows somewhat with
horizon (Section~\ref{sec:continuation}). This supports using the
canonical LRU-continuation labels with reasonable confidence in the
evaluated regimes, not a claim that continuation choice is irrelevant.

\textbf{What capacity-scale reuse tells you before running anything.}
Trace-derived LRU hit rate at the target capacity is the statistic most
strongly associated with both offline informativeness and closed-loop
policy separation in this $n=8$--10 exploratory evidence. It is therefore
a useful screening signal for where to look next, not a guarantee of what
a new trace or deployed policy will do.
