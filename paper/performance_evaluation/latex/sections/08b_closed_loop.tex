\section{Closed-Loop Policy Evaluation (RQ2, part 1)}
\label{sec:closed-loop}

\begin{sloppypar}
Section~\ref{sec:characterization} characterizes the offline target.
This section reports the actual closed-loop cache-policy behavior it will
be compared against in Section~\ref{sec:linkage}
(Table~\ref{tab:tier1-closed-loop}). Scope: 5 families
$\times$ 2 capacities (32, 128) $\times$ 4 policies (LRU, MRU, random-mean

Section~\ref{sec:experimental-methodology}.

\textbf{LRU is the best policy in 9 of 10 cells.} The sole exception is
metakv/cap128, where SIEVE beats LRU by 4.22\% relative. Conversely,
\textbf{MRU is the worst policy in every non-degenerate cell} by a wide
margin. Random-policy seed variability is small (maximum standard
deviation 0.0025), which is sufficient to reliably separate wide gaps but
means very narrow differences in low-signal cells (like metakv/cap32) fall within
the stochastic noise floor. No universal ranking holds for SIEVE vs.\ random;
their relative ordering is strictly family-dependent.

The traces cluster into three regimes. First, \textbf{wiki2018 is completely degenerate},
with a 1.0 miss ratio and exactly zero standard deviation for all policies, following
deductively from its lack of reuse (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). Second,
\textbf{alibaba-block is a near-tie regime} where all policy gaps remain small. Third,
the highly discriminative families (metacdn and twemcache) show massive separation
between LRU and MRU. The metakv/cap128 cell remains the only genuine exception to LRU's
dominance, where both SIEVE and random outperform it. As noted in Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-design},
metacdn's results derive from validation-window evidence, while the rest are
test-window.
