\section{Closed-Loop Policy Evaluation (RQ2, part 1)}
\label{sec:closed-loop}

\begin{sloppypar}
Section~\ref{sec:characterization} characterizes the offline target.
This section reports the actual closed-loop cache-policy behavior it will
be compared against in Section~\ref{sec:linkage}
(Table~\ref{tab:tier1-closed-loop}). Scope: 5 families
$\times$ 2 capacities (32, 128) $\times$ 4 policies (LRU, MRU, random-mean
over 20 seeds, SIEVE~\cite{zhang2024sieve}), per
Section~\ref{sec:experimental-methodology}.
\end{sloppypar}

\textbf{LRU is the best of the four Tier-1 comparators in 9 of 10 cells.}
The sole exception is metakv/cap128, where SIEVE beats LRU by 4.22\% relative.
Conversely,
\textbf{MRU is the worst policy in every non-degenerate cell} by a wide
margin. Random-policy seed variability is small (maximum standard
deviation 0.0025), which is sufficient to reliably separate wide gaps but
means very narrow differences in low-signal cells (like metakv/cap32) fall within
the stochastic noise floor. No universal ranking holds for SIEVE vs.\ random;
their relative ordering is strictly family-dependent.

The traces cluster into three regimes. First, \textbf{wiki2018 is completely degenerate},
with a 1.0 miss ratio and exactly zero standard deviation for all policies, following
deductively from its lack of reuse (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). Second,
\textbf{alibaba-block is a near-tie regime} where all policy gaps remain small. Third,
the highly discriminative families (metacdn and twemcache) show massive separation
between LRU and MRU. The metakv/cap128 cell remains the only genuine exception to LRU's
dominance, where both SIEVE and random outperform it. As noted in Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-design},
metacdn's results derive from validation-window evidence, while the rest are
test-window.

\paragraph{Expanded comparator robustness.}
Table~\ref{tab:tier1-closed-loop} compares four policies; to check that its
conclusions are not an artifact of that narrow set, we re-ran the identical
Tier-1 protocol with three additional deterministic comparators --
ARC~\cite{megiddo2003arc}, LIRS~\cite{jiang2002lirs}, and
S3-FIFO~\cite{yang2023s3fifo} (full results:
Table~\ref{tab:expanded-closed-loop}). Under the expanded seven-policy set,
LRU is strictly best in 4 of the 10 cells rather than 9 of 10, but five of
the six cells in which another policy now leads LRU do so by less than 3.2\%
relative (0.45\%, 0.91\%, 1.29\%, 1.68\%, 3.19\%) -- comparable to gaps this
paper already treats as near-ties -- so most of the reordering is fine-grained
jockeying among already-close policies. The substantive exception remains
metakv/cap128, where S3-FIFO now beats LRU by 8.07\% relative (versus SIEVE's
4.22\% under the original four). Importantly for
Section~\ref{sec:linkage}, the association between offline discriminativeness
and closed-loop policy spread (max$-$min miss ratio across the compared
policies) is unchanged at $r=0.96$ under either the four-policy or the
seven-policy spread definition ($n=10$ family$\times$capacity cells,
exploratory), so the offline$\leftrightarrow$closed-loop correspondence is not
an artifact of the original comparator set. W-TinyLFU was preregistered for
this expansion but excluded after its reference implementation proved
non-functional at capacity 32 under its own default configuration
(\texttt{COMPARATOR\_PROTOCOL.md}); it contributes no results here.
