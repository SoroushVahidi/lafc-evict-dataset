\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Caching Infrastructure and Benchmarks}
Cache studies rely on realistic traces and reusable tooling. The UMass Trace Repository and production workload studies provide traces for evaluation~\cite{umasstracerepo,atikoglu2012kvworkload,yang2020twemcache}. CacheLib, libCacheSim, and cache\_dataset provide cache-engine simulation~\cite{berg2020cachelib,libcachesim,cachedataset}, while broader systems artifacts like YCSB and Park show the value of reusable learning-augmented evaluation infrastructure~\cite{cooper2010ycsb,difallah2013oltpbench,leis2018job,mao2019park}.

\subsection{Learned and Adaptive Replacement}
Recent learned-caching work typically contributes a policy, policy-selection scheme, or learning framework. LRB, LHD, GL-Cache, and 3L-Cache focus on low-overhead or feature-rich per-object eviction~\cite{song2020lrb,beckmann2018lhd,yang2023glcache,zhou2025threelcache}. LeCaR, CACHEUS, MAT, and ALPS form another cluster of adaptive policies~\cite{vietri2018lecar,rodriguez2021cacheus,yang2023mat,shahout2024alps}, while HR-Cache, Parrot, Hawkeye, and Mockingjay further explore eviction decisions~\cite{torabi2024hrcache,liu2020parrot,jain2016hawkeye,shah2022mockingjay}. Baleen addresses the adjacent admission/prefetching axis~\cite{wong2024baleen}.

\subsection{Heuristics and Learning-Augmented Theory}
Classical and modern heuristics define the landscape \lafc\ interfaces with, ranging from recency/frequency-aware policies (LIRS, ARC, CLOCK-Pro, GreedyDual-Size~\cite{jiang2002lirs,megiddo2003arc,jiang2005clockpro,cao1997greedydual}) to lightweight admission-aware designs (TinyLFU, S3-FIFO, SIEVE, LAH~\cite{einziger2017tinylfu,yang2023s3fifo,zhang2024sieve,xia2026lah}). Learning-augmented caching theory studies algorithms with predictions and robustness guarantees~\cite{lykouris2021mladvice,wei2020learningaugcache,chledowski2021robustlac,im2022parsimoniouslac,chen2026robustpaging}. \lafc\ does not supply algorithmic guarantees, but asks how reusable per-decision supervision can be constructed and characterized. The subsampling study of Wang et al. identifies extremely long decision horizons as a practical obstacle for reinforcement learning~\cite{wang2019subsampling}, motivating our focus on finite-horizon supervision.

\subsection{Offline versus Deployed Evaluation}
Offline and deployed behavior need not agree when actions affect future observations. DAgger formalizes this distribution-shift problem for imitation learning~\cite{ross2011dagger}, and similar gaps are documented in recommender systems~\cite{castells2022offline} and caching throughput optimizations~\cite{qiu2026hitratio}. This paper asks a specific question in this space: whether finite-horizon counterfactual miss-cost gaps align with closed-loop miss-ratio gaps for cache eviction.

\subsection{Positioning \lafc}
\label{sec:positioning}

Given this literature, \lafc's contribution is a public cache-eviction
artifact that releases reusable candidate-level finite-horizon
counterfactual supervision, together with decision-aligned candidate, decision,
and pairwise task views, at production scale and decoupled from any particular
downstream learned method. To clarify \lafc's novelty boundaries relative to the
closest prior works, we distinguish it on several axes:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Ch\l{}edowski et al. \cite{chledowski2021robustlac}} provide an experimental study of robust learning-augmented caching and release valuable workload/data resources. \lafc\ differs in artifact granularity and intended use: rather than evaluating online policies with predictions under theoretical bounds, it materializes reusable candidate-level finite-horizon counterfactual miss costs plus derived decision/pairwise views, and systematically evaluates their correspondence with closed-loop behavior.
  \item \textbf{Cache-Coliseum \cite{cachecoliseumrepo}} provides a comprehensive algorithm and execution benchmark for evaluating learning-augmented caching policies. In contrast, \lafc\ is a data-centric supervision benchmark providing static, pre-computed counterfactual labels. The two resources benchmark different objects: Cache-Coliseum benchmarks policy execution frameworks, while \lafc\ packages the standardized label surface those policies can train or be audited against.
  \item \textbf{HALP \cite{song2023halp}} leverages preference-based learning for eviction, where preference signals are generated and consumed online during policy training and deployment. In contrast, \lafc\ provides a static, materialized offline dataset of counterfactual $y_{\text{loss}}$ labels and a deterministic pairwise preference view derived directly from it, reusable independently of a particular deployed learned policy.
  \item \textbf{Belady's look-ahead optimum \cite{belady1966study}} represents the theoretical full-future offline-optimal policy. In contrast, \lafc's primary supervision target ($y_{\text{loss}}$) represents the candidate-level finite-horizon counterfactual miss cost under a specified continuation policy. This distinction is conceptually crucial, as the local finite-horizon "optimal set" under a continuation policy is not equivalent to Belady's full-future global optimal choice.
\end{itemize}

Ultimately, \lafc\ does not claim to propose a new online eviction policy, nor to be the first use of future-access supervision, preference learning, or benchmarking in this space. Rather, its novelty lies in providing a public, reusable, candidate-level finite-horizon counterfactual supervision artifact at production scale; supplying aligned candidate, decision, and pairwise views; systematically characterizing when the target is informative; conducting continuation-policy robustness analysis; and providing a direct empirical comparison against closed-loop policy behavior.
