\subsection{Infrastructure, Traces, and Benchmarks}

Cache studies rely on realistic traces and reusable tooling. The UMass
Trace Repository, production workload studies, CacheLib, libCacheSim, and
cache\_dataset provide traces, cache-engine context, and simulation support
for trace-driven evaluation~\cite{umasstracerepo,atikoglu2012kvworkload,yang2020twemcache,berg2020cachelib,libcachesim,cachedataset}.
Broader systems artifacts such as YCSB, OLTP-Bench, Join Order Benchmark,
and Park show the value of reusable evaluation infrastructure for systems
and learning-augmented systems research~\cite{cooper2010ycsb,difallah2013oltpbench,leis2018job,mao2019park}.
Most directly, Cache-Coliseum is a NeurIPS 2025 benchmark for evaluating
learning-augmented caching algorithms, with multiple algorithm
implementations and predictors~\cite{chen2025guard,cachecoliseumrepo}.
\lafc\ therefore does not claim to be the first cache benchmark for
learned or learning-augmented caching; its narrower claim is a different
artifact type: reusable per-decision, per-candidate finite-horizon
counterfactual supervision, plus decision and pairwise derived views.

\subsection{Learned and Adaptive Cache Replacement}

Recent learned-caching work typically contributes a policy, policy-selection
scheme, or learning framework. LRB, LHD, GL-Cache, 3L-Cache, LeCaR,
CACHEUS, MAT, ALPS, HR-Cache, Parrot, Hawkeye, Mockingjay, and HALP differ
in model class, target signal, and deployment setting, but they all study
how to make a cache choose better online or with lower overhead
~\cite{song2020lrb,beckmann2018lhd,yang2023glcache,zhou2025threelcache,vietri2018lecar,rodriguez2021cacheus,yang2023mat,shahout2024alps,torabi2024hrcache,liu2020parrot,jain2016hawkeye,shah2022mockingjay,song2023halp}.
HALP is especially close to \lafc's pairwise view because it trains from
preference comparisons between candidate evictions in production, but its
signal is generated and consumed online by a deployed policy. \lafc's
pairwise view is instead a static reformatting of already-materialized
finite-horizon counterfactual labels. Baleen addresses the adjacent
admission/prefetching axis rather than per-candidate eviction labels
~\cite{wong2024baleen}.

\subsection{Heuristics and Learning-Augmented Caching}

Classical and modern policies define the policy landscape that \lafc\
interfaces with: Belady's look-ahead optimum, LIRS, ARC, CLOCK-Pro,
GreedyDual-Size, TinyLFU/W-TinyLFU, S3-FIFO, lazy-promotion/quick-demotion,
SIEVE, and LAH/S4-FIFO all emphasize different practical signals or
robustness/overhead tradeoffs~\cite{belady1966study,jiang2002lirs,megiddo2003arc,jiang2005clockpro,cao1997greedydual,einziger2017tinylfu,einziger2018wtinylfu,yang2023s3fifo,yang2023lazypromotion,zhang2024sieve,xia2026lah}.
Section~\ref{sec:expanded-comparators} empirically tests three of these
(ARC, LIRS, S3-FIFO) under \lafc's closed-loop protocol, to check whether
its closed-loop conclusions are specific to the smaller LRU/MRU/random/SIEVE
comparator set used elsewhere in this paper.
Learning-augmented caching theory instead studies algorithms with
predictions and robustness/consistency guarantees
~\cite{lykouris2021mladvice,wei2020learningaugcache,chledowski2021robustlac,im2022parsimoniouslac}.
The subsampling study of Wang et al. identifies extremely long decision and
reward horizons as a practical obstacle for reinforcement-learning cache
policies and uses subsampling to reduce that horizon~\cite{wang2019subsampling};
this motivates treating temporal horizon as a first-class difficulty, but
does not validate \lafc's particular $H\in\{4,8,16\}$ choices.

\subsection{Offline versus Deployed Evaluation}

Offline and deployed behavior need not agree when actions affect future
observations. DAgger formalizes this distribution-shift problem for
imitation learning and structured prediction; \lafc\ does not implement
DAgger, but its closed-loop checks are motivated by the same caution
~\cite{ross2011dagger}. In recommender systems, Castells and Moffat
similarly survey why offline evaluation should not be equated with online
utility~\cite{castells2022offline}. Within caching, Qiu, Yang, and
Harchol-Balter show a cache-local metric can even move opposite to
end-to-end performance: beyond a point, higher hit ratio can reduce
throughput because it changes hit-path contention~\cite{qiu2026hitratio}.
This paper asks a narrower question -- whether finite-horizon
counterfactual miss-cost gaps align with closed-loop miss-ratio gaps -- and
reports a bounded, regime-characterized answer.

\subsection{Positioning \lafc}

Given this literature, \lafc\ is not a new online eviction policy, not the
first use of future accesses for supervision, not the first preference
learning approach for eviction, and not the first benchmark for
learning-augmented caching. To the best of our knowledge, its distinct
contribution is a public cache-eviction artifact that releases reusable
candidate-level finite-horizon counterfactual supervision, together with
decision-level and pairwise task views, at production scale and decoupled
from any particular downstream learned method. It complements traces,
simulators, Cache-Coliseum-style algorithm benchmarks, learned/adaptive
policies, and learning-augmented theory by supplying reusable labels for
offline value regression, best-candidate selection, preference learning,
and label characterization.
