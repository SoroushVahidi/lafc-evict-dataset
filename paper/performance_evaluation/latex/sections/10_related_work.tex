\subsection{Infrastructure, Traces, and Benchmarks}

Cache studies rely on realistic traces and reusable tooling. The UMass
Trace Repository and production workload studies provide realistic traces
for trace-driven evaluation~\cite{umasstracerepo,atikoglu2012kvworkload,yang2020twemcache}.
CacheLib, libCacheSim, and cache\_dataset provide cache-engine context and
simulation support for the same
purpose~\cite{berg2020cachelib,libcachesim,cachedataset}.
Broader systems artifacts such as YCSB, OLTP-Bench, Join Order Benchmark,
and Park show the value of reusable evaluation infrastructure for systems
and learning-augmented systems research~\cite{cooper2010ycsb,difallah2013oltpbench,leis2018job,mao2019park}.
Most directly, Cache-Coliseum is a NeurIPS 2025 benchmark for evaluating
learning-augmented caching algorithms, with multiple algorithm
implementations and predictors~\cite{chen2025guard,cachecoliseumrepo}. It
benchmarks algorithms against each other; \lafc\ instead supplies the
labels an algorithm would train or evaluate against -- reusable
per-decision, per-candidate finite-horizon counterfactual supervision,
plus decision and pairwise derived views (full positioning in
Section~\ref{sec:positioning}).

\subsection{Learned and Adaptive Cache Replacement}

Recent learned-caching work typically contributes a policy,
policy-selection scheme, or learning framework; the systems below differ
in model class, target signal, and deployment setting, but all study how
to make a cache choose better online or with lower overhead. LRB, LHD,
GL-Cache, and 3L-Cache are one such
cluster~\cite{song2020lrb,beckmann2018lhd,yang2023glcache,zhou2025threelcache}.
Within this cluster, 3L-Cache specifically targets the computational-overhead
barrier to deploying a learned per-object eviction policy, using
bidirectional sampling and parameter auto-tuning to cut CPU cost while
remaining a per-object policy rather than a reusable supervision
artifact~\cite{zhou2025threelcache}.
LeCaR, CACHEUS, MAT, and ALPS are
another~\cite{vietri2018lecar,rodriguez2021cacheus,yang2023mat,shahout2024alps}.
HR-Cache, Parrot, Hawkeye, and Mockingjay round out this
group~\cite{torabi2024hrcache,liu2020parrot,jain2016hawkeye,shah2022mockingjay}.
HALP~\cite{song2023halp} takes the same general approach and is discussed
further below for its similarity to \lafc's pairwise view.
HALP is especially close to \lafc's pairwise view because it trains from
preference comparisons between candidate evictions in production, but its
signal is generated and consumed online by a deployed policy. \lafc's
pairwise view is instead a static reformatting of already-materialized
finite-horizon counterfactual labels. Baleen addresses the adjacent
admission/prefetching axis rather than per-candidate eviction labels
~\cite{wong2024baleen}.

\subsection{Heuristics and Learning-Augmented Caching}

Classical and modern policies define the policy landscape that \lafc\
interfaces with. Belady's look-ahead optimum is the offline-optimal
reference point against which all of the below are
measured~\cite{belady1966study}. LIRS, ARC, CLOCK-Pro, and GreedyDual-Size
are classical recency/frequency-aware
policies~\cite{jiang2002lirs,megiddo2003arc,jiang2005clockpro,cao1997greedydual}.
TinyLFU, W-TinyLFU, and S3-FIFO are more recent lightweight,
admission-aware
designs~\cite{einziger2017tinylfu,einziger2018wtinylfu,yang2023s3fifo}.
Lazy-promotion/quick-demotion, SIEVE, and LAH/S4-FIFO round out this
group, each emphasizing a different robustness/overhead
tradeoff~\cite{yang2023lazypromotion,zhang2024sieve,xia2026lah}.
LAH departs from per-object scoring by instead learning the cache-level
parameters of a static heuristic (S3-FIFO, as S4-FIFO) with an
asynchronous control-plane/data-plane split; \lafc\ does not propose a
replacement policy or controller at either granularity, and instead
exposes the counterfactual supervision such controllers could be trained
or evaluated against~\cite{xia2026lah}.
Learning-augmented caching theory instead studies algorithms with
predictions and robustness/consistency guarantees
~\cite{lykouris2021mladvice,wei2020learningaugcache,chledowski2021robustlac,im2022parsimoniouslac}.
Recent work in this line narrows the robustness gap for
learning-augmented paging to within an additive constant of the optimal
competitive ratio~\cite{chen2026robustpaging}; \lafc\ does not supply
such a guarantee -- it instead asks how reusable per-decision supervision
for eviction choices can be constructed and characterized, a
complementary rather than competing question.
The subsampling study of Wang et al. identifies extremely long decision and
reward horizons as a practical obstacle for reinforcement-learning cache
policies and uses subsampling to reduce that horizon~\cite{wang2019subsampling};
this motivates treating temporal horizon as a first-class difficulty, but
does not validate \lafc's particular $H\in\{4,8,16\}$ choices.

\subsection{Offline versus Deployed Evaluation}

Offline and deployed behavior need not agree when actions affect future
observations. DAgger formalizes this distribution-shift problem for
imitation learning and structured prediction; \lafc\ does not implement
DAgger, but its closed-loop checks are motivated by the same caution
~\cite{ross2011dagger}. In recommender systems, Castells and Moffat
similarly survey why offline evaluation should not be equated with online
utility~\cite{castells2022offline}. Within caching, Qiu, Yang, and
Harchol-Balter show a cache-local metric can even move opposite to
end-to-end performance: beyond a point, higher hit ratio can reduce
throughput because it changes hit-path contention~\cite{qiu2026hitratio}.
This paper asks a narrower question -- whether finite-horizon
counterfactual miss-cost gaps align with closed-loop miss-ratio gaps -- and
reports a bounded, regime-characterized answer.

\subsection{Positioning \lafc}
\label{sec:positioning}

Given this literature, \lafc's contribution is a public cache-eviction
artifact that releases reusable candidate-level finite-horizon
counterfactual supervision, together with decision-level and pairwise task
views, at production scale and decoupled from any particular downstream
learned method. It complements traces, simulators, Cache-Coliseum-style
algorithm benchmarks, learned/adaptive policies, and learning-augmented
theory by supplying a reusable label surface for offline value regression,
best-candidate selection, preference learning, and label characterization
-- not a new online eviction policy, and not a claim to be the first use
of future-access supervision, preference learning, or benchmarking in this
space (scope stated once here rather than repeated per subsection above).
