\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Reuse}
\label{sec:limitations}

This evaluation surface has several important constraints, and the
practical guidance in Section~\ref{sec:practical-implications} should be
read together with them rather than instead of them.

First, the main label is a finite-horizon, continuation-policy-specific
counterfactual miss cost, not an offline-optimal label for the full future
control problem. Good performance on this label does not automatically
imply online deployment gains, which is exactly why
Sections~\ref{sec:closed-loop} and~\ref{sec:linkage} evaluate closed-loop
behavior directly rather than relying on the offline label alone.

Second, the canonical labels use LRU as the continuation policy after a
forced eviction. Section~\ref{sec:continuation} reports two bounded
continuation-policy checks: a sampled study at capacities 32 and 128,
where both MRU and mean-random continuation classify as robust on the
pre-registered rule, and a full-population MRU census across capacities
32, 64, 128, and 256, which also satisfies the MRU robustness criterion.
This does not prove continuation-independence in general. Random
continuation is not validated at population scale, and no experiment here
covers every possible stateful deployment policy.

Third, the target is substantially tied. Across the full audited canonical
dataset, the all-tied fraction is approximately 0.68 and the unique-winner
fraction is exactly 0.0 -- no audited decision in this dataset has a single
uniquely-best candidate victim. Discriminativeness varies strongly by
family, capacity, and horizon; one
family, wiki2018, is a fully degenerate negative control at every
capacity and horizon tested, with zero reuse events in its scored windows,
which is a mathematical certainty rather than a statistical property of
that trace.

Fourth, the evaluated dataset uses unit-object, count-based capacities (32,
64, 128, or 256 objects) with unconditional admission of the requested
object. Variable object sizes, byte-weighted miss cost, latency-aware cost,
and dirty-write cost are not modeled by this label family, and admission
is never withheld. Claims in this paper should be read as scoped to this
abstraction and not generalized to byte-addressable or admission-gated
cache models without further evidence.

Fifth, closed-loop evidence itself has two family-specific caveats that
must be carried alongside any result that uses it. MetaCDN's closed-loop
evidence is validation-window evidence, not a temporally held-out test
window (Twemcache's is test-window). MetaKV is the only family whose
scored window begins at an empty cache (a cold start, identifiable from
the identity evictions = misses - capacity); this is plausibly, but not
conclusively, connected to MetaKV being the one Tier-1 cell where LRU
falls to third place, behind both SIEVE and random-mean
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}).

Sixth, a known data-generation issue remains open: in the audited canonical
data, \texttt{candidate\_is\_predictor\_victim} is identical to
\texttt{candidate\_is\_lru\_victim} throughout, and related predictor/
bucket/confidence fields include constant or placeholder values. This
paper does not make any claim that depends on those predictor fields being
informative.

Seventh, benchmark reuse must preserve provenance distinctions. The release
exposes generated supervision and derived views, but it does not erase the
attribution, citation, or licensing requirements of the upstream raw traces
from which candidate rows were derived; those raw traces remain external
artifacts with their own governance constraints. The open subset is also
intentionally narrower than the full generated corpus for redistribution
reasons, and not every selected family appears in every split of the
evaluated release package. The exact generator commit for the full
historical release corpus is not completely reconstructed; this is
orthogonal to the audited evidence reported in this paper but is disclosed
here for reproducibility completeness.

Finally, this paper reports closed-loop results for LRU, MRU, random, and
SIEVE (Section~\ref{sec:closed-loop}); it does not report a closed-loop
evaluation of any learned policy trained on these labels. A frozen pilot
(not the production Tier-1 evaluation reported here) previously found a
simple learned policy competitive with random and SIEVE but not with LRU
on one family, with the other pilot family blocked on the absence of an
independent test window; neither result is repeated or extended here, and
learned-policy closed-loop competitiveness remains future work rather than
a claim of this paper.
