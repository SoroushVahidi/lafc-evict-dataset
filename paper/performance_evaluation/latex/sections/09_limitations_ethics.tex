\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Reuse}
\label{sec:limitations}

This evaluation surface has several important constraints, and the
practical guidance in Section~\ref{sec:practical-implications} should be
read together with them rather than instead of them.

First, the main label is a finite-horizon, continuation-policy-specific
counterfactual miss cost, not an offline-optimal label for the full future
control problem. Good performance on this label does not automatically
imply online deployment gains, which is exactly why
Sections~\ref{sec:closed-loop} and~\ref{sec:linkage} evaluate closed-loop
behavior directly rather than relying on the offline label alone.

Second, horizon-dependent results have two scopes. The reusable release
uses $H\in\{4,8,16\}$. The extended sensitivity experiment (Section~\ref{sec:characterization})
evaluates $H\in\{16,32,64,128\}$ on a matched physical decision population
of 787,762 decisions to ensure strict consistency across horizons, using
existing H16 evidence and completed long-horizon high-performance computing (HPC)
outputs, but it does not change the public release contents.

Third, the canonical labels use LRU continuation after a forced eviction;
Section~\ref{sec:continuation}'s sampled and full-population studies
establish robustness only within their tested scope -- random continuation
is not validated at population scale, and no experiment covers every
deployment policy.

Fourth, the target is substantially tied (all-tied fraction
$\approx$0.68, unique-winner fraction 0.0; Section~\ref{sec:characterization}
quantifies this by family, capacity, and horizon, and
Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms} gives the causal explanation for wiki2018's
complete degeneracy).

Fifth, the evaluated dataset uses unit-object, count-based capacities (32,
64, 128, or 256 objects) with unconditional admission of the requested
object. Variable object sizes, byte-weighted miss cost, latency-aware cost,
and dirty-write cost are not modeled by this label family, and admission
is never withheld. Claims in this paper should be read as scoped to this
abstraction and not generalized to byte-addressable or admission-gated
cache models without further evidence.

Sixth, closed-loop evidence carries two family-specific caveats, defined
in Section~\ref{sec:experimental-methodology} and revisited in
Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}: metacdn's evidence is validation-window, not
test-window, and metakv's scored window begins at a cold start, plausibly
linked to metakv's one Tier-1 policy-ranking exception.

Seventh, a known data-generation issue remains open: in the audited
canonical data, the predictor-victim flag is identical to the LRU-victim
flag throughout -- an exact redundancy, not merely a correlation -- and
related predictor, bucket, and confidence fields include constant or
placeholder values -- specifically, 18 of the 26 candidate-level feature
columns used by the pairwise feature-difference model
(Section~\ref{sec:tasks-baselines}) are hard-coded constants across the
entire release. Together with the redundant predictor-victim flag, 19 of
the 26 supplied features provide no additional independent variation,
leaving seven that are nonconstant and nonredundant. This paper does not
make any claim that depends on those predictor fields, or the seven
remaining features, being informative beyond nonconstancy.

Eighth, benchmark reuse must preserve provenance distinctions. The release
exposes generated supervision and derived views, but it does not erase the
attribution, citation, or licensing requirements of the upstream raw traces
from which candidate rows were derived; those raw traces remain external
artifacts with their own governance constraints. The currently hosted
open subset is also narrower than the full generated corpus, but for two
distinct reasons that should not be conflated: four of the five evaluated
families (alibaba-block, metacdn, metakv, twemcache) are cleared for
public redistribution as of a 2026-09-15 review but are not yet packaged
into a hosted public release, a packaging backlog rather than a licensing
limitation, while two further families (brightkite, citibike) remain
excluded for unresolved redistribution reasons (see the Data Availability
statement for current clearance and hosting status). Not every selected
family appears in every split of the evaluated release package. The full
historical release corpus's generator-commit provenance is also
incomplete, orthogonal to the audited evidence reported here
(Section~\ref{sec:release-validation} gives the exact scope).

Finally, this paper reports closed-loop results for LRU, MRU, random, and
SIEVE under the Tier-1 protocol (Section~\ref{sec:closed-loop}), and,
separately, a frozen learned-policy closed-loop check under an
independently constructed held-out protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:learned-closed-loop}). That check covers one model
class, one feature set, one target ($H=16$ \texttt{y\_loss}), and two
capacities; it found no consistent closed-loop improvement over LRU,
SIEVE, or random, and should be read as a single confirmatory result, not
an exhaustive study of learned-policy architectures, targets, or tuning,
and not a general claim about learned cache eviction beyond this frozen
configuration.
