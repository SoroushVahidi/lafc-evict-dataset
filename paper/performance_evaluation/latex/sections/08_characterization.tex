\section{Target Discriminativeness (RQ1)}
\label{sec:characterization}

This section answers RQ1: is the finite-horizon counterfactual target
discriminative between candidates, and when does it degenerate? Source:
\texttt{analysis/sigmod\_target\_discriminativeness\_20260913/}, audited
over all 277,995,072 candidate rows / 2,363,286 decisions in the canonical
dataset (all capacities, all horizons; the full population, not a sample).

\subsection{The uncomfortable aggregate facts, stated first}

Pooled across every family, capacity, and horizon: the decision-weighted
random-optimal probability is 0.9912, the decision-weighted mean random
regret is 0.0088, the all-tied fraction is 0.6766, and the unique-winner
fraction is exactly 0.0. In plain terms: a uniformly random candidate
choice lands in the optimal set 99.1\% of the time, over two-thirds of
decisions have every candidate exactly tied, and \emph{no audited decision
in this dataset has a single, strictly-best candidate victim}. The overall
classification, by the audit's own pre-registered rule, is
\textbf{B~=~CONDITIONALLY DISCRIMINATIVE} -- not uniformly discriminative,
and this is stated here as the paper's own finding rather than a limitation
discovered post hoc. Aggregate degeneracy this large is, by itself, a
substantive result about the target's shape.

\subsection{Where the target is informative}

The aggregate picture, however, is not the same at every family, capacity,
and horizon -- consistent with prior large-scale production evidence that
cache workloads vary substantially in access pattern and reuse structure
even within a single deployment~\cite{yang2020twemcache}, which is why
this section reports per-family results rather than only the pooled
aggregate above. Figure~\ref{fig:discriminativeness} shows discriminativeness
(1 -- all-tied fraction) across all five families, four capacities, and
three horizons (source data: same audit, per-stratum breakdown,
\path{outputs/phase4_stratified_family_capacity_horizon.csv}, 60
family$\times$capacity$\times$horizon cells). Two families,
\textbf{metacdn} and \textbf{twemcache}, are substantially more
discriminative than the aggregate figure suggests -- at $H=16$, metacdn's
all-tied fraction is 0.0085--0.012 and twemcache's is 0.10--0.24 across its
two smallest capacities, an order of magnitude below the aggregate 0.68.
\textbf{alibaba-block} and \textbf{metakv} sit at the tie-heavy end
(all-tied fraction 0.66--0.87 at $H=16$), closer to but still below the
degenerate extreme. \textbf{wiki2018} is exactly at the degenerate
extreme: all-tied fraction 1.0 at every capacity and every horizon tested,
with zero reuse events in its scored windows (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}
shows this is a mathematical certainty, not a statistical property of the
trace, and treats wiki2018 as a genuine negative control rather than a
benchmark failure). Discriminativeness also increases with horizon within
every non-degenerate family (e.g.\ twemcache/cap32 rises from 0.46 at
$H=4$ to 0.76 at $H=16$) -- a longer look-ahead window resolves more ties,
consistent with more future requests giving more opportunities for
candidates' fates to diverge.

The extended long-horizon comparison asks whether this picture changes
when the look-ahead window grows beyond the release horizons.
Table~\ref{tab:matched-long-horizon} and
Figure~\ref{fig:matched-long-horizon} summarize the validated
$H\in\{16,32,64,128\}$ comparison. This uses the exact same canonical
physical eviction-decision population of 787,762 decisions across all horizons,
removing any aggregate denominator mismatch. On this matched population,
aggregate discriminativeness increases monotonically with the look-ahead horizon
(pooled discriminative fraction rising from 0.3807 at $H=16$ to 0.4566 at $H=128$),
while the aggregate all-tied fraction decreases monotonically and expected random
regret increases. Thus the changes diminish between H64 and H128, but a
saturation claim would be too strong: effects remain heterogeneous by family
and capacity. The target also remains strongly tie-degenerate at H128:
the decision-micro all-tied fraction is 0.5434.

\textbf{Look-ahead relative to cache capacity ($H/C$).} To investigate
whether the high tie mass is merely an artifact of look-ahead horizons
being short relative to cache capacity, we analyze the cell-level $H/C$
ratios across the matched-population dataset, spanning $H/C \in [0.0625, 4.0]$.
Substantial degeneracy remains in evaluated cells where $H$ equals or exceeds
cache capacity, so the high tie mass cannot be attributed solely to using
horizons shorter than the cache: the pooled all-tied fraction is 57.14\% at
$H/C = 1.0$, 60.45\% at $H/C = 2.0$, and 63.83\% at $H/C = 4.0$. Because $H/C$
buckets contain different capacity/horizon compositions, these pooled $H/C$
differences should not be interpreted as a causal effect of $H/C$ itself, but
rather as evidence that family and capacity composition remain important and
that $H \ge C$ regimes are not inherently tie-free.

Additionally, we distinguish between cumulative raw regret and normalized
regret per request ($\text{regret}/H$). On the exact matched 787,762-decision
population, raw random regret rises monotonically with $H$ (0.0132 at $H=16$,
to 0.0361 at $H=128$), but the aggregate normalized regret per request decreases
monotonically: 0.000826 at $H=16$, 0.000653 at $H=32$, 0.000453 at $H=64$, and
0.000282 at $H=128$. For example, for metacdn/cap32 ($H/C$ rising from 0.5 to
4.0), raw random regret rises from 0.0601 ($H=16$) to 0.0894 ($H=128$), but the
normalized regret per request drops from 0.00375 to 0.00070. Conversely,
the most tie-heavy families like alibaba-block and metakv remain
consistently low-information and low-regret across all horizons. This
indicates that longer look-ahead horizons accumulate more raw opportunity
for regret mechanically, but do not increase the per-request target
informativeness proportionally.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The scientific contribution here is partly \emph{characterizing} where the
target is informative rather than asserting it is informative everywhere.
High tie rates are not hidden or explained away: they are the majority
regime in this dataset, and Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms} connects them
directly to trace-level reuse structure (capacity-scale locality), giving
a mechanistic account of exactly why metacdn/twemcache are discriminative
and alibaba-block/metakv/wiki2018 are not. Sections~\ref{sec:closed-loop}
and~\ref{sec:linkage} then ask whether this same discriminativeness
structure predicts anything about actual closed-loop cache-policy
behavior (it does, in the regimes identified here).
