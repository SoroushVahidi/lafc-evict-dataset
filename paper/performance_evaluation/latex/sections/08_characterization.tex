\section{Target Discriminativeness (RQ1)}
\label{sec:characterization}

This section answers RQ1: is the finite-horizon counterfactual target
discriminative between candidates, and when does it degenerate? Source:
\texttt{analysis/sigmod\_target\_discriminativeness\_20260913/}, audited
over all 277,995,072 candidate rows / 2,363,286 decisions in the canonical
dataset (all capacities, all horizons; the full population, not a sample).

\subsection{The uncomfortable aggregate facts, stated first}

Pooled across every family, capacity, and horizon: the decision-weighted
random-optimal probability is 0.9912, the decision-weighted mean random
regret is 0.0088, the all-tied fraction is 0.6766, and the unique-winner
fraction is exactly 0.0. In plain terms: a uniformly random candidate
choice lands in the optimal set 99.1\% of the time, over two-thirds of
decisions have every candidate exactly tied, and \emph{no audited decision
in this dataset has a single, strictly-best candidate victim}. By the
audit's pre-registered rule, the target is
\textbf{conditionally, not uniformly, discriminative}. Degeneracy this
large is itself a substantive result about the target's shape, not a
caveat to work around.

\subsection{Where the target is informative}

The aggregate picture, however, is not the same at every family, capacity,
and horizon -- consistent with prior large-scale production evidence that
cache workloads vary substantially in access pattern and reuse structure
even within a single deployment~\cite{yang2020twemcache}, which is why
this section reports per-family results rather than only the pooled
figure above. Figure~\ref{fig:discriminativeness} shows discriminativeness
(1 -- all-tied fraction) across all five families, four capacities, and
three horizons (source data: same audit, per-stratum breakdown,
\texttt{outputs/phase4\_stratified\_family\_capacity\_horizon.csv}, 60
family$\times$capacity$\times$horizon cells). Two families,
\textbf{metacdn} and \textbf{twemcache}, are substantially more
discriminative than the aggregate figure suggests -- at $H=16$, metacdn's
all-tied fraction is 0.0085--0.012 and twemcache's is 0.10--0.24 across its
two smallest capacities, an order of magnitude below the aggregate 0.68.
\textbf{alibaba-block} and \textbf{metakv} sit at the tie-heavy end
(all-tied fraction 0.66--0.87 at $H=16$), closer to but still below the
degenerate extreme. \textbf{wiki2018} is exactly at the degenerate
extreme: all-tied fraction 1.0 at every capacity and every horizon tested,
with zero reuse events in its scored windows (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}
shows this is a mathematical certainty, not a statistical property of the
trace, and treats wiki2018 as a genuine negative control rather than a
benchmark failure). Discriminativeness also tends to increase with horizon
within non-degenerate families: a longer look-ahead window resolves more
ties, consistent with more future requests giving more opportunities for
candidates' fates to diverge. For horizon sensitivity, we use the exact
same canonical physical eviction-decision population at
$H\in\{16,32,64,128\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:matched-long-horizon} and
Figure~\ref{fig:matched-long-horizon}). This removes the denominator
ambiguity from an earlier aggregate audit: every row has 787,762
decisions, and the H16 row matches the canonical release exactly. On this
matched population, pooled mean random regret rises from 0.0132 at $H=16$
to 0.0361 at $H=128$, while all-tied mass falls from 0.619 to 0.543.
Horizon length is not the whole explanation, however: at $H=128$, more
than half of decisions remain all-tied, wiki2018 remains exactly all-tied
at every capacity, and MetaKV remains mostly low-information.

\subsection{Does informativeness translate into method separation?}
\label{sec:informativeness-utility}

To answer whether the benchmark can distinguish methods when the decision
contains useful eviction information, we pre-registered a decision
informativeness statistic before evaluating selector outcomes:
\[
  E[\mathrm{regret}\mid\mathrm{uniform\ random}]
  = \mathrm{mean}_c(\texttt{y\_loss}(c)) - \min_c \texttt{y\_loss}(c).
\]
This statistic is zero exactly for all-tied decisions and is measured in
misses. We keep those zero-information decisions as stratum Z. For
positive-information decisions, we assign quartiles within each
family$\times$capacity$\times$horizon cell and then pool corresponding
quartile ranks (Q1--Q4); this prevents one workload or capacity scale from
defining the thresholds. The protocol and scripts are in
\texttt{analysis/problem3\_informativeness\_20260917/}; the protocol was
committed before the selector-outcome pass.

The resulting pattern is conditional rather than universal. In stratum Z,
every method has exactly zero regret and 100\% optimal-set selection, which
is the desired behavior: when all candidates are equivalent under the
finite-horizon objective, the benchmark should not manufacture separation.
As expected random regret increases, uniform random regret rises from
0.0144 (Q1) to 0.0492 (Q4). LRU and the clean pairwise selector are much
better than random in every positive-information stratum (mean regret
0.0005--0.0341), and their optimal-set selection rates remain above 0.967
even in Q4. The clean linear and frozen HGB pointwise selectors, by
contrast, are worse than random in every positive-information stratum;
this is a useful failure signal rather than something hidden by ties.

The separation is not a monotone success story. LRU and clean pairwise gain
most over random in Q3 (about 0.025 misses) and less in Q4 (about 0.015
misses), because the highest-information bin includes regimes where
non-optimal choices are more costly but the LRU-like feature signal is also
less reliable. Family-level robustness confirms the condition: MetaCDN,
MetaKV, and most lower-informativeness non-wiki strata show strong
LRU/pairwise separation from random, while alibaba-block and twemcache Q4
are harder and can sharply reduce or reverse the LRU advantage. Therefore
\lafc\ is useful as a regime-conditioned benchmark: it distinguishes
reasonable tie-aware selectors from random and from weaker learned
pointwise selectors where the finite-horizon counterfactual signal is
informative, but it is not uniformly discriminative across all
workload/capacity regimes.

The headline random-optimal probability is also less catastrophic than it
sounds. Of all decisions, 67.66\% are zero-information and contribute no
random regret. Another 31.66\% have very large optimal sets (optimal-set
fraction at least 0.90) and at least one worse candidate; these account
for 86.8\% of total expected random regret but still have mean
random-optimal probability 0.976. Only 0.68\% of decisions fall into the
material-regret-separation group (expected random regret above 1/16
misses), yet they contribute 13.2\% of total expected random regret. Thus
the 99.12\% random-optimal statistic is dominated by decisions where
alternative choices are exactly or nearly equivalent under the benchmark
objective, not by a failure to identify meaningful regimes.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The scientific contribution here is partly \emph{characterizing} where the
target is informative rather than asserting it is informative everywhere.
High tie rates are not hidden or explained away: they are the majority
regime in this dataset. The informativeness-stratified utility result
separates two facts that the aggregate random-optimal statistic conflates:
low-information decisions correctly show little or no method separation,
while positive-information regimes can distinguish LRU-like and tie-aware
selectors from both random choice and weaker pointwise learned selectors.
Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms} connects this structure to trace-level reuse
(capacity-scale locality), and Sections~\ref{sec:closed-loop}
and~\ref{sec:linkage} then ask whether the same discriminativeness
structure predicts actual closed-loop cache-policy behavior (it does, in
the regimes identified here).
