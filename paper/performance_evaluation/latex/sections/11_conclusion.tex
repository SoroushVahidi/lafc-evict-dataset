\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper positions \lafc\ as a benchmark for learned cache eviction, releasing candidate-level counterfactual supervision alongside derived decision and pairwise tasks. The open release enables researchers to train and evaluate policies against finite-horizon miss costs at scale, without requiring repetitive derivation of labels from raw traces.

We characterize this target empirically, revealing that it is frequently tie-degenerate but predictably discriminative based on trace-level reuse structures. Crucially, in regimes where the offline signal separates candidates, offline policy rankings correspond positively with true closed-loop miss-ratio performance. This correspondence is robust to variations in the assumed continuation policy (tested against MRU and random alternatives) and family-aware statistical bounds.

However, offline performance does not guarantee closed-loop success. As demonstrated by a frozen learned-policy check, predicting the offline target well does not automatically yield closed-loop cache-miss reductions over robust heuristics like LRU and SIEVE. \lafc\ explicitly embraces this mismatch, emphasizing that static offline supervision complements, rather than replaces, closed-loop evaluation. Future work should explore on-policy training, trajectory-aware objectives, and richer architectures designed to robustly bridge this predict-then-act distribution shift.
