\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper positions \lafc\ as a benchmark for learned cache eviction rather than as a new policy paper. The core contribution is a release-oriented package built around candidate-level counterfactual supervision, derived decision and pairwise tasks, and validation-aware construction. The open release couples manifest-backed scale and end-to-end validation with summaries of candidate-label distributions, a lightweight linear value-regression baseline, a derived best-candidate selector, and pairwise-sample-backed feature baselines that show measurable decision-relevant signal in the released candidate features.

\begin{sloppypar}
Returning to the five questions posed in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: the
offline target is discriminative in a workload-, capacity-, and
horizon-dependent way rather than uniformly, with one trace family serving
as a mechanistically explained negative control (RQ1). Offline candidate
rankings and closed-loop cache performance agree in most workload/capacity
regimes where the offline signal is not near zero, with two specific,
characterized exceptions (RQ2). Capacity-scale reuse in the underlying
trace correlates with both offline discriminativeness and closed-loop
policy separation, most strongly at the longest of the three horizons
in the reusable release ($H=16$ of $H\in\{4,8,16\}$). The extended
$H\in\{16,32,64,128\}$ sensitivity check shows diminishing changes by
H64--H128 while preserving the main conclusion that the target remains
substantially tied even at H128 (RQ3, exploratory, $n=10$ for the
closed-loop linkage). The canonical LRU-continuation labels are not
an artifact of that specific continuation choice within the tested scope
(RQ4, detailed below). Taken together, these findings support concrete,
scope-limited guidance on when the offline signal can be trusted in
practice (RQ5, Section~\ref{sec:practical-implications}). A separate,
frozen learned-policy closed-loop check (Section~\ref{sec:learned-closed-loop})
puts that guidance to a direct test: the model did not consistently
out-perform LRU, SIEVE, or random under its own held-out protocol, which
is itself informative -- offline supervision quality on this target does
not automatically translate into closed-loop gains for this frozen
configuration.
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{sloppypar}
Those results warrant conservative interpretation. The linear regression baseline is weak, the best-candidate selector is a reproducible benchmark floor rather than deployment evidence, the pairwise results remain backed by the shipped capped sample rather than any full quadratic pairwise universe, and the labels remain finite-horizon counterfactuals rather than globally optimal online targets. The validated continuation studies substantially reduce concern that the canonical LRU continuation alone drives the reported target ordering, but only within the tested scope: sampled MRU/random at capacities 32/128 and population MRU at capacities 32/64/128/256. The frozen learned-policy check is similarly a single confirmatory point, not an exhaustive study of learned-policy design space; it does not license re-tuning against the same held-out cells used to obtain it. Future extensions can instead focus on alternative training targets, tie-aware objectives, other learned architectures evaluated on fresh held-out data, policies designed explicitly for the closed-loop state distribution a policy induces, online policy studies that sit above the benchmark layer, broader continuation policies, and broader public hosting where redistribution constraints permit it.
\end{sloppypar}
