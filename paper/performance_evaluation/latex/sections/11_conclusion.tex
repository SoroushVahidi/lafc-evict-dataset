\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper positions \lafc\ as a benchmark artifact for learned cache eviction rather than as a new policy paper. The core contribution is a release-oriented package built around candidate-level counterfactual supervision, derived decision and pairwise tasks, and validation-aware artifact construction. The preserved open release now couples manifest-backed scale and successful end-to-end validation with committed summaries of candidate-label distributions, a lightweight linear value-regression baseline, a derived best-candidate selector, and pairwise-sample-backed feature baselines that show measurable decision-relevant signal in the released candidate features.

\begin{sloppypar}
Returning to the five questions posed in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: the
offline target is discriminative in a workload-, capacity-, horizon-, and
decision-informativeness-dependent way rather than uniformly: low-information
decisions correctly show no method separation, positive-information regimes
distinguish LRU-like/tie-aware selectors from random and weaker pointwise
learned selectors, and one trace family serves as a mechanistically
explained negative control (RQ1). Offline candidate rankings and
closed-loop cache performance agree in most workload/capacity
regimes where the offline signal is not near zero, with two specific,
characterized exceptions (RQ2). Capacity-scale reuse in the underlying
trace correlates with both offline discriminativeness and closed-loop
policy separation, most strongly at the longest of the three horizons
used in the main released decision population ($H=16$ of
$H\in\{4,8,16\}$); a matched-population H16--H128 audit shows horizon
length increases informativeness but does not eliminate low-information
regimes (RQ3, exploratory, $n=10$). The canonical
LRU-continuation labels are not
an artifact of that specific continuation choice within the tested scope
(RQ4, detailed below). Taken together, these findings support concrete,
scope-limited guidance on when the offline signal can be trusted in
practice (RQ5, Section~\ref{sec:practical-implications}).
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{sloppypar}
Those results warrant conservative interpretation. The linear regression
and frozen HGB pointwise selectors are weak under the decision-level regret
audit, the pairwise logistic result is useful but closely tracks LRU-like
features, the pairwise sample remains capped rather than a full quadratic
pairwise universe, and the labels remain finite-horizon counterfactuals
rather than globally optimal online targets. The validated continuation
studies substantially reduce concern that the canonical LRU continuation
alone drives the reported target ordering, but only within the tested
scope: sampled MRU/random at capacities 32/128 and population MRU at
capacities 32/64/128/256. Future extensions can focus on stronger
tie-aware learned baselines, online policy studies that sit above the
benchmark layer, broader continuation policies, and broader public hosting
where redistribution constraints permit it.
\end{sloppypar}
