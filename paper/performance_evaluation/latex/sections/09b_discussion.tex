\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What \lafc\ enables.} It provides a reusable, candidate-level, finite-horizon counterfactual supervision surface. This allows researchers to explore value regression, best-candidate selection, and pairwise preference learning without repeatedly re-deriving labels from raw traces. As positioned in Section~\ref{sec:related-work}, this public scale and granularity are distinct from existing benchmarking efforts.

\textbf{What the evaluation reveals.} Four critical insights emerge from evaluating the surface against closed-loop behavior. First, target discriminativeness is highly workload-structured, not incidental noise (Section~\ref{sec:characterization}). Second, this structure is mechanically tied to capacity-scale reuse (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). Third, offline policy orderings successfully predict closed-loop performance in most informative regimes, though notable exceptions exist (Section~\ref{sec:linkage}). Fourth, the canonical LRU-continuation assumption is robust within the evaluated capacity bounds (Section~\ref{sec:continuation}).

\textbf{The role of degenerate regimes.} Cases like wiki2018's complete degeneracy and alibaba-block's near-zero signal are fully explained results, not anomalies. Understanding exactly why and where the target degenerates makes the non-degenerate claims trustworthy and provides a crucial diagnostic tool for researchers.

\textbf{Offline surrogate vs.\ closed-loop validation.} This paper's central position is that offline counterfactual supervision is complementary to, not a replacement for, closed-loop evaluation. As our frozen learned-policy check demonstrated (Section~\ref{sec:learned-closed-loop}), offline predictive structure does not automatically yield closed-loop cache-miss gains, because predict-then-act path dependence fundamentally alters future trajectories. \lafc\ therefore explicitly embraces offline-to-online correspondence testing as a necessary, integrated part of evaluating learned eviction strategies.
