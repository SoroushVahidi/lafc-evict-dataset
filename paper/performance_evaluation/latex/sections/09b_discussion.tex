\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The four preceding sections evaluate the same counterfactual label from
four angles: whether it discriminates between candidates
(Section~\ref{sec:characterization}), why its discriminativeness varies by
workload (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}), whether it agrees with actual
closed-loop cache behavior (Section~\ref{sec:linkage}), and whether it
depends on the LRU-continuation assumption used to generate it
(Section~\ref{sec:continuation}). None of these four questions can be
answered by describing the label alone; each required either a real
closed-loop replay, an independent trace-level measurement, or a relabeling
experiment.

The offline$\leftrightarrow$closed-loop correspondence is the paper's
central empirical contribution, not the offline label by itself: the two
discordant cells in Section~\ref{sec:linkage} were found by the same
procedure that found the eight agreeing ones, not selected after the fact,
and both are mechanistically investigated rather than smoothed over
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}). Similarly, wiki2018's complete degeneracy
and alibaba-block/cap32's near-zero offline signal are explained, not
merely observed (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}) -- understanding why a
result is degenerate is what makes the non-degenerate results trustworthy.

Section~\ref{sec:limitations} collects the full scope of every claim above;
the load-bearing ones for this discussion are the target's substantial tie
mass, the small sample size ($n=8$--10) behind the linkage correlations,
and the tested-but-not-exhaustive scope of continuation policies
(Section~\ref{sec:continuation}).
