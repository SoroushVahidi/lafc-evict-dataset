\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What LAFC-Evict enables.} A reusable, candidate-level, finite-horizon
counterfactual supervision surface, materialized once from real traces and
usable across value-regression, best-candidate-selection, and pairwise
formulations (Sections~\ref{sec:background}--\ref{sec:tasks-baselines})
without every study re-deriving its own labels from raw traces
(Section~\ref{sec:related-work} situates this against prior learned-cache
work, most of which derives labels internally and non-reusably).

\textbf{What the evaluation reveals, beyond the surface itself.} Four
findings that only emerge from evaluating the surface against closed-loop
behavior, not from describing the surface alone: the target's
discriminativeness is workload-structured, not incidental
(Section~\ref{sec:characterization}); that structure is explained, not
just observed, by a single trace-level property, capacity-scale reuse
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}); offline and closed-loop policy orderings
agree in most, not all, informative regimes, with the exceptions
identified rather than smoothed over (Section~\ref{sec:linkage}); and the
canonical LRU-continuation assumption is robust, with quantified limits,
at the capacities tested (Section~\ref{sec:continuation}).

\textbf{Why degenerate regimes are useful.} wiki2018's complete degeneracy
and alibaba-block/cap32's near-zero offline signal are explained results
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}), not unexplained anomalies. Mechanistically
understood degenerate cases make the non-degenerate claims more
trustworthy and answer the earlier criticism that tie-heaviness was stated
but not investigated.

\textbf{Offline surrogate evaluation vs.\ closed-loop performance.} This
paper's position, supported by Sections~\ref{sec:linkage}
and~\ref{sec:closed-loop}, is that the offline$\leftrightarrow$closed-loop
correspondence itself -- not the offline surrogate alone -- is the
contribution: the two exceptions found were identified by the same
symmetric procedure that found the 8 agreeing cells, not selected for
balance, and are characterized rather than smoothed over
(Section~\ref{sec:practical-implications} gives the resulting practitioner
guidance in detail).

\textbf{Continuation robustness as currently validated.} The robustness
finding in Section~\ref{sec:continuation} matters because it addresses a
specific threat to the label's validity: that the canonical LRU
continuation, not the eviction choice itself, is what actually drives the
reported target ordering. Within its tested scope
(Section~\ref{sec:practical-implications} gives the exact capacities and
numbers), it is not: switching continuation policy leaves the ordering
essentially unchanged. This is a statement about the tested alternatives,
not a claim that every stateful deployed continuation policy would
preserve the same ordering.

\textbf{Methodological limits} are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. For this discussion, the load-bearing ones
are target discriminativeness, small-$n$ linkage evidence, and the tested
scope of continuation policies.
